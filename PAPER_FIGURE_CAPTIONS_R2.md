# MATLAB figure captions and interpretation limits

Use only the figures under `results/release_20261113_r2/figures/`. A 25-page report should select the figures needed for its argument; all 17 figures need not appear in the main text. Throughout, “building exposure” means the model's building-clearance proxy, not observed building failures.

1. **F1 — Shoreline evolution.** Effective beach width under the S2 once-only intervention, seed 42. The dashed lines bound the central 5 km reporting corridor within the 9 km computational domain; negative raw widths are displayed as zero. This is a conditional model scenario.
2. **F2 — Equal-volume placement layouts.** Beach-width profiles at years 5 and 15 for uniform, hotspot, and multiple-segment placements with the same available sand volume and shared S1 storms. The 20 m line is the management width threshold.
3. **F3 — Strategy exposure.** Fifteen-year beach-width and building-clearance exposures for the S2 and S3 baseline and intervention strategies, seed 42. These are individual realizations; rankings require the paired Monte Carlo analysis and actual sand/cost information.
4. **F4 — Storm uncertainty.** Medians and pointwise 10th–90th percentiles of building-clearance exposure and terminal beach width under S2. Parameters are fixed; the intervals describe storm realizations, not all parameter or structural uncertainty. Baseline and adaptive results are shown separately from the three other active strategies for readability.
5. **F5 — One-at-a-time sensitivity.** Range of seed-averaged building-clearance exposure across the prescribed values of each input. Input ranges differ, so bar lengths are response ranges rather than standardized derivatives or variance contributions.
6. **F6 — Width and clearance.** Shared-storm S2 trajectories of mean effective beach width and mean building clearance, seed 42. Repeated timestamps preserve instantaneous event and placement jumps; width and clearance are distinct outcomes.
7. **F7 — Sand budget verification.** Cumulative placement and reconstructed full-domain balance, with relative residuals for the full 9 km domain and central 5 km corridor. The deficit correction accounts for continued shoreline retreat after the mobile dry beach is exhausted; mass closure is a numerical check, not field validation.
8. **F8 — Storm timing.** Exposure medians and 10th–90th percentiles for the same six event marks rearranged into early, spread, and late sequences. Differences reflect timing, width-dependent storm erosion, recovery and threshold effects; the experiment does not assume early storms must maximize cumulative risk.
9. **F9 — Initial coverage feasibility.** Analytical initial-volume threshold for uniform placement to make every location meet the width criterion. This diagram concerns instantaneous coverage and does not establish durability under erosion or storms.
10. **F10 — Historical diagnostic.** Shoreline-change rates for the 101 transects selected by a fixed NAD83 UTM18N northing window. The top panel compares overlapping periods. The bottom panel fits the reduced Gaussian net-rate field on 1978–1989 and holds out 1989–2002; the periods share the 1989 shoreline, so errors can be correlated. This does not validate the full PDE or offshore reservoir.
11. **F11 — Global sensitivity.** First- and total-order Sobol estimates from independent assumed input ranges, N=512 and two common storm seeds, varying one input group while holding other groups fixed. Storm-group changes retain the specified chronic-compensation rule and are therefore not pure storm-only perturbations. Consult `sensitivity_convergence.csv` for nested sample sizes and independent-scramble diagnostics. Unstable or out-of-range estimates are retained and must not be interpreted as precise variance shares.
12. **F12 — Where the intervention's sand-volume effects appear.** Paired intervention-minus-baseline changes in beach storage, nearshore storage, boundary export, offshore loss and current deficit at year 15. These are signed counterfactual effects, not tagged-grain probabilities; avoided native-sand deficit is included explicitly.
13. **F13 — Spatiotemporal uncertainty.** Pointwise 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of effective width under S2 once-only intervention at half-year reporting times. Each quantile surface is a statistical summary, not a mass-conserving realization or a simultaneous confidence band.
14. **F14 — Durability phase map.** Mean length-weighted safe years for uniform and hotspot placements across initial minimum width and available sand volume, using 32 common S1 storm sequences per point. A length-weighted safe year is the time integral of safe coastline fraction, not a guarantee that the entire corridor is safe for one year.
15. **F15 — Broader uncertainty and policy approximations.** Exposure quantiles under storm-only versus assumed parameter-plus-storm uncertainty, and under five adaptive-policy settings sharing 100 realized storm seeds. The refined policy uses 16 forecast members and a 50 m/0.05 yr forecast grid; monitoring costs and nearshore measurement noise are assumptions, not measured local values.
16. **F16 — Adaptive decisions and monitoring.** Realized S2 project completions after permitting and construction delays, and predictive discrepancies at 6/12/24-month synthetic surveys, seed 42. A discrepancy above 30% triggers an approximate weight update; this demonstration is not an empirical parameter posterior, and mismatch can also arise from storm or structural error.
17. **F17 — Storm footprint assumptions.** S2 exposure medians and 10th–90th percentiles under uniform regional storms and peak-normalized Gaussian footprints of widths 1, 2 and 5 km, with 100 matched storm-mark seeds per fixed strategy. Natural-event centers are uniform across the computational domain and shared across widths; the forced stress event remains centered at zero. Peak normalization changes integrated erosion, so this is not an equal-integrated-energy comparison. The dedicated experiment supplies explicit event tables rather than relying on a YAML footprint switch.

## Definitions to retain in the report

- Exposures are `E_c = integral integral 1(margin_c<0) dx dt`, in m·yr, over the central 5 km.
- `T_safe,cum = integral (1-f_c(t)) dt = T-E_c/L`, in length-weighted years. `T_first,fail` instead uses the first time failed length fraction reaches 0.5.
- Unobserved first failures are right-censored; null does not mean zero or infinite protection.
- Natural recovery is censored by another storm, nourishment or the simulation horizon. Its pooled KM summary is descriptive because samples are dependent and nourishment-related censoring can be informative.
- A `retreat_assessment` flag requests comparison with alternatives; no relocation/buyout model has established an optimum.
- S2's fixed-actual-retreat event is a state-conditioned stress test. S3's sea-level and chronic-retreat endpoints are separate scenario assumptions; no local sea-level-to-retreat conversion or separate storm-water-level baseline is inferred.
